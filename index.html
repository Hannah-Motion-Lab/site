Hannah

An AI companion that lives on your desktop. She listens, answers in her own voice, and moves while she talks — on your own hardware.

Install on Linux

Free and open source · runs on your GPU · nothing leaves your machine by default

She is a presence, not a chat window.

You talk. She answers out loud.

Speech recognition and a neural voice run locally, so there is no round trip to a server between your sentence and hers. Interrupt her mid-sentence and she stops and listens.

Whisper for listening, Kokoro for the voice. Sub-second from the end of your sentence to the start of hers.

She moves while she speaks.

Every sentence is turned into body language by a text-to-motion model we trained ourselves, and mapped onto her skeleton from geometry rather than guesswork. She gestures, shifts her weight, looks at you.

Eight facial expressions, lip-sync per syllable, and a gaze that follows your cursor across monitors.

She lives on the desktop, not in a tab.

A small window that stays on top of everything, on every workspace, and remembers where you left it. Say “go to the other screen” and she does. Works the same on GNOME, KDE, XFCE and Hyprland.

400×620 by default. Close the window and the whole stack shuts down and frees your GPU.

She can see, and she can act.

With the camera on, a local vision model describes what is in front of her and she reacts only when something actually changes. Turn on tools and she opens apps, runs commands in a real terminal, and searches the web — anything destructive asks you first.

For jobs that take several steps — “organize my downloads by type” — she hands the work to a separate agent and narrates what it does. The agent never speaks; she is the only voice.

Tools, terminal and the agent are all off by default. Teach her new skills with a markdown file.

One command.

The installer sets up everything on your machine: the model server and models, the voice, the gesture model, the overlay, and a hannah command to bring it all up.

curl -fsSL https://hannah-motion-lab.github.io/site/install.sh | bash
Then
hannah starts her. hannah doctor tells you what your desktop supports. hannah stop shuts everything down.
Size
About 20 GB once the models are in place. They are built and downloaded on your machine, not bundled.
Platforms
Linux x86_64 today. Windows and macOS builds exist in the repository and are untested.
Prefer to build from source?

Clone the workspace and its four repositories side by side, then follow SETUP.md — it is the same steps the installer runs, written out.

git clone https://github.com/Hannah-Motion-Lab/workspace.git Hannah-Motion
cd Hannah-Motion
git clone https://github.com/Hannah-Motion-Lab/backend.git      hannah-backend
git clone https://github.com/Hannah-Motion-Lab/frontend.git     hannah-frontend
git clone https://github.com/Hannah-Motion-Lab/motion-model.git hannah-motion-lab
git clone https://github.com/Hannah-Motion-Lab/desktop.git      hannah-desktop
cat SETUP.md

What she needs.

GPUNVIDIA with CUDA, up to 16 GB of VRAM for the full stack. AMD or CPU-only also works; the voice gets slower.
BrainOllama running qwen2.5:7b, with nomic-embed-text for long-term memory and moondream for vision. The installer pulls all three.
SystemLinux x86_64, Node 20+, Python 3.12+. The installer handles the rest.
DesktopAny: the overlay runs under XWayland so it can stay on top everywhere. wmctrl or kdotool let you move her by voice.
PrivacyAudio never touches disk and transcripts never touch logs. The only thing that leaves your machine is the optional agent’s model, which you enable knowingly.
